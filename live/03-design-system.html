North star: two screens decide whether this product is any good — Onboarding (do I trust this?) and Room (do I stay?). Everything else is scaffolding. Design those two until they are boringly obvious, then make the rest match.

1. Eight principles

# Principle In practice What it forbids
1 One decision per screen Home has one primary button; room has one centre control Split CTAs, carousels, "while you're here"
2 Calm is a feature Neutral surfaces, one accent used for state only Gradients, glassmorphism, confetti, glow
3 Show presence, not status Speaking circles, connection dot, balance bar Followers, badges, levels-as-trophies, "top speaker"
4 The interface disappears during speech Controls fade to 25% opacity after 6s of no touch; a tap restores Persistent chrome over a conversation
5 Motion explains, never decorates 140ms ease-out on state change; sheets 220ms Bounces, parallax, auto-playing loops
6 Accessible by default AA contrast, 48px targets, screen-reader live regions, no colour-only meaning Icon-only unlabelled controls, colour as the only signal
7 Never punish leaving No confirmation, no penalty, no guilt copy Streak-loss warnings, "wait, don't go"
8 Text is UI Sentences instead of labels where a sentence is clearer Jargon: "room", "stage", "club", "host" in the interface

Note principle 8: internally we say room and table. In the interface we say "Talk", "Practise with someone", "Join a table" — words a learner would use.

2. Foundations

2.1 Typography

One family. Two weights. No display face.

Token Size / line Weight Use
display 32 / 38 600 Welcome headline, recap total
title 22 / 28 600 Screen titles
body 17 / 26 400 Everything readable
label 15 / 20 500 Buttons, chips
meta 13 / 18 400 Timers, pair labels, captions
micro 12 / 16 500 Tabular only: seconds, counts
  • Family: system stack (-apple-system, Segoe UI, Inter) — no font download, no layout shift, native feel, and it is the calmest possible choice.
  • Numerals in timers and the balance meter are tabular so digits do not jitter.
  • Body never below 17px in the room; 15px is the floor anywhere.
  • Max line length 38 characters on mobile. If a sentence needs more, it is two sentences.

2.2 Colour

One accent. Semantic colour only for state. Dark is the default; light is a first-class theme, not a filter.

Token Dark Light Use
bg #0B0C0E #FBFBF9 Canvas
surface #141619 #FFFFFF Cards, sheets
line #24272C #E5E4DF Dividers, borders
ink #F2F1EC #141518 Primary text
muted #9AA0A6 #6B7076 Secondary text
signal #3DDC97 #0F7A5A Live, connected, speaking
warm #F2B705 #8A5F00 Correction cue, gentle warning
alert #FF6B5A #B4402E Destructive, report, error

Rules: - Never use signal for decoration. If it is green, something is live or connected. - warm appears at most once per screen — it is the "this is a learning moment" colour. - No colour carries meaning alone: mute always pairs colour with an icon and a label change. - Contrast: body text ≥ 7:1, meta ≥ 4.5:1, large text ≥ 3:1.

2.3 Spacing and shape

  • Base unit 4px. Scale: 4 · 8 · 12 · 16 · 24 · 32 · 48 · 64.
  • Screen gutter 20px; card padding 16px; vertical rhythm between blocks 24px.
  • Radius: cards 16, sheets 24 (top corners), pills 999, circles 50%.
  • Elevation: one shadow token, used only for sheets. No stacked shadows, no borders and shadows on the same element.

2.4 Motion

Interaction Duration Curve
Press feedback 90ms ease-out
State change (mute, connect) 140ms cubic-bezier(.2,.8,.2,1)
Sheet present/dismiss 220ms same
Presence pulse while speaking 1.6s loop, 4% scale ease-in-out
  • prefers-reduced-motion → every animation becomes a 120ms cross-fade; the presence pulse becomes a static ring.
  • Nothing animates for longer than 1.6s except the speaking pulse, and that pulse must be interruptible by touch.

That is the entire vocabulary. If a new screen needs a new component, the design is probably wrong.

4. The room screen, wireframed in words

This is the screen the company is judged on.

Top (safe area + 16px): English ↔ Spanish on the left in meta/muted; 04:12 on the right in micro/tabular. Below them, a 6px dot + the word Connected — green when healthy, amber for "unstable", red for "reconnecting". Tapping the dot opens a one-line diagnosis sheet: "Your connection is weak. Move somewhere quieter or switch to audio-only."

Middle (vertically centred, 40% of height): Two presence circles, 88px, 40px apart. You on the left, partner on the right. The circle of whoever is speaking expands to 104px over 1.6s and its ring fills. Under each: a 13px name, muted. No photos.

Directly beneath: the balance bar, 240px wide, two segments — yours in signal, theirs in muted. It is the only element that can turn warm, and only after 90 seconds of one-sided speech.

Lower third: A prompt card, collapsed to a single line: "Ask about a job they want." A small chevron expands it to three prompts. It auto-collapses 60 seconds into the call and never returns unless tapped.

Bottom (thumb zone, 20px gutter, 24px above safe area): Three circles on one row, evenly spaced: Leave (56, ghost, left) · Mute (72, filled, centre) · More (56, ghost, right).

Mute is the only filled control in the room. When muted, it fills alert-tinted neutral, the icon becomes a struck mic, and a 13px label under it reads Muted — three signals, none of them colour alone.

More sheet: Correct my Spanish · Change topic · Report · Block. Report and Block are separated by 16px and sit at the bottom, in alert, never adjacent to a positive action.

What is deliberately absent: no participant list, no room title bar with a count, no "share", no emoji bar, no reaction animations, no waveform visualiser. A waveform is the fastest way to make a calm screen noisy.

5. Onboarding, wireframed in words

Second north-star screen. Under 60 seconds, five taps, one sentence per screen (08 §4).

Screen Layout Copy (exact)
1 Centred, one line of type at 32/38, one illustration (two circles, one speaking), one primary button "Speak a language with a real person. Right now." · sub: "No profiles. No scheduling. Ten minutes is enough." · button: Get started
2 Title + two stacked buttons + 13px footnote "How old are you?" · I'm 18 or older · I'm under 18 (disabled with reason) · footnote: "We only match adults. Under-18 access is coming with a different safety model."
3 Two chip rows, max 6 visible, "more" expands "What do you speak?" then "What do you want to practise?" · Only pairs with live supply are selectable; the rest show "no one online right now"
4 Big record button, live level ring, one sentence to read "Say this: 'I'm preparing for a job interview next month.'" · after 20s: "Sounds like B1. Change?" with a ± stepper and a Looks right button
5 Four chips, 2×2 "What are you practising for?" · Job interview · Everyday conversation · Exam · Just talking
6 Mic level meter, playback, one primary "Let's check your mic." · Sounds good · ghost: Fix audio
→ Straight to Home, no tour, no permissions carousel Home shows one card: "14 people online for English ↔ Spanish" and one button: Talk

Onboarding rules: - No account creation before the first room. The sign-in prompt appears after the recap, titled "Save your progress". - No notification permission request during onboarding. It is asked after the first completed room, with a reason. - Every screen is skippable except the age gate. - The estimated level is always editable and never shown as a judgment ("Sounds like B1" not "You are B1").

6. Accessibility, concretely

Requirement Implementation
Screen reader Presence circles expose aria-live="polite": "Mateo is speaking", "You are speaking", "Muted"
Mute state Icon + label + colour + haptic on toggle
Touch targets 48px minimum everywhere; 56/72 in-call
Contrast Body 7:1; all interactive text 4.5:1
Dynamic type Layout tested to 200%; room controls reflow to two rows rather than overlap
Captions Optional live caption strip, off by default, on-device; toggled in the More sheet
Reduced motion Cross-fades only; speaking state conveyed by ring stroke, not scale
Motor No drag-only actions; every gesture has a button equivalent
Cognitive One primary action; plain-language copy; no timers that punish

7. Anti-patterns (do not ship these)

  1. A follower count, even in settings.
  2. A profile photo required to be matched.
  3. A "room full" state — cap at six and show a table instead.
  4. Emoji reactions floating over the conversation.
  5. A waveform visualiser during the call.
  6. Confetti on a streak.
  7. "Rate this conversation" with five stars.
  8. A countdown on the free-tier cap during a call. Show remaining time on Home only; never interrupt a conversation.
  9. A red badge on the app icon.
  10. A "share your recap" prompt. Recaps are private until the user chooses otherwise.
