Five risks can kill this. They are ordered by how quickly they do it, not by how likely they are.

1. Safety incident (existential)

What it looks like: a minor in an adult room, a sexual-harassment recording that circulates, or a credible threat made live. One screenshot is enough to end the company — the product's own premise (stranger voice) is the evidence.
Likelihood Medium over 24 months at scale, low in a 200-person beta
Impact Existential — brand, app-store removal, legal exposure, personal harm
Leading indicator Report rate per 1,000 room-minutes; share of reports mentioning a minor; retention of women in cohorts
Mitigation 18+ hard gate; phone before room 1, liveness before room 3; no identifiers or location; one-tap report that ends the call; 60s device-local evidence buffer; 24/5 human review; 2 FTE at launch; permanent, device-level bans
Residual risk We cannot detect intent. Verification proves accountability, not character.
Kill / freeze signal Any confirmed minor in an adult room, or any CSAM: stop matching immediately, external audit, no growth until the audit closes

The honest position: this risk is reduced, not eliminated. Any live voice product with strangers carries it. The design choice is to make the company accountable for it — named reviewers, published enforcement times, and a public safety report every quarter — rather than pretending moderation solved it.

2. Cold-start liquidity (the most likely killer)

What it looks like: you open the app, wait 40 seconds, get "no one online", and never come back. Every language pair needs simultaneous supply on both sides, in the same timezone, at the same level band. That is a hard constraint that marketing cannot fix.
Likelihood High — this is the default outcome for two-sided voice products
Impact Fatal if unresolved; the product simply does not function
Leading indicator P50/P90 time-to-match; "% of taps that end in a completed room"; AI-fallback rate
Mitigation Launch one pair only, in one time-zone corridor, during four concentrated evening hours; unpaid founding speakers (free Career, three sessions/week) instead of paid hosts; AI fallback so the queue is never empty; reciprocity minutes for "giver" sessions; tables only after 1:1 liquidity is proven
Cost of mitigation Recruiting 600 founding speakers (300 per side) and two campus ambassadors per city: ~$3–5k/month. No payments for presence — a paid host creates a two-class room and does not scale
Kill signal P90 time-to-match > 45s after 8 weeks of seeding → the "instant" promise is false. Action: narrow to one pair, or convert to scheduled practice tables (honest pivot, not a redesign)

Why AI fallback is not the answer to liquidity: it protects the individual experience but destroys the network value. If 40% of minutes are AI, users learn the product is a chatbot with extra steps. Target: AI fallback ≤ 15% of minutes; if it exceeds 20% for two weeks, liquidity is failing and seeding must increase or the pair must be cut.

3. Audio quality (the silent churn)

What it looks like: one side hears "you're breaking up", the call ends politely, and neither user reports it. Churn looks like disinterest; it is actually jitter.
Likelihood High on mobile networks, especially in Brazil, India, and Southeast Asia
Impact High — a language product that cannot be heard has no product
Leading indicator Call setup success; reconnect rate; self-reported clarity; % of calls < 3 minutes
Mitigation SFU with regional edges (LiveKit/Daily/Agora), Opus 24–32 kbps, jitter buffer tuning, pre-join network probe with a warning, 1:1 P2P fallback when SFU latency spikes, automatic downgrade to lower bitrate before dropping, "audio is bad" one-tap diagnostic that routes to a support queue
Target ≥ 97% call setup; MOS ≥ 4.2; < 2% of calls end due to audio
Kill signal < 90% call setup after two optimisation cycles → the target markets are not ready; restrict launch geographies

4. Retention: live audio is a novelty (the Clubhouse lesson)

What it looks like: week-one usage is strong, week-two collapses. Clubhouse had tens of millions of users and no durable habit; live audio alone is not a reason to return.
Likelihood Medium-high — this is the category's documented failure mode
Impact High — no retention, no business, regardless of unit economics
Leading indicator Second room within 7 days; D7/D30; sessions per active week; recap open rate
Mitigation The learning loop is the retention product: recap with a concrete next step, word bank, weekly goal (not daily streak), goal-based rooms (interview in 3 weeks), and "your partner said hello" re-match prompts — not push-notification spam
Why this might work where Clubhouse failed Clubhouse sold presence; this sells progress against a dated goal. The recap is the artefact that says "you did something", which a stage never produced
Kill signal D7 < 20% at week 6 of beta, or second-room-within-7-days < 30% → live audio is not the habit. Action: re-scope to scheduled practice tables or goal-based cohorts; do not add features to a product nobody returns to

5. Business: moderation and verification costs scale faster than revenue

What it looks like: every new user costs ~$0.90 to verify and a share of a reviewer's time, while only 3–5% ever pay. Growth increases the loss.
Likelihood Medium
Impact Medium — survivable, but it caps growth and forces a raise
Leading indicator Cost per verified user; cost per room-minute; reports per 1,000 room-minutes; gross margin per paid user
Mitigation Verification only before room 3 (not at install); re-verification only on suspicion; reviewer tooling that triages by pattern rather than reading every report; tighter free cap if COGS drifts; annual plans to fund verification upfront
Target Blended cost per free MAU < $0.50/month; paid gross margin > 90%
Kill signal Blended gross margin < 70% at 50k MAU → the model does not work at this price; reprice or restrict free tier to 10 min/day

Risk summary

# Risk Likelihood Impact Earliest signal Hard action
1 Safety incident Medium Existential Minor-related reports Freeze matching, external audit
2 Liquidity High Fatal P90 match time Narrow pairs or pivot to tables
3 Audio quality High High Call setup %, < 3 min calls Restrict geographies, change SFU
4 Retention Medium-high High D7, second room in 7 days Re-scope to goal-based cohorts
5 Cost structure Medium Medium Cost per room-minute Reprice or cut free cap

The pattern: risks 2–4 all present as "people don't come back." The only way to tell them apart is instrumentation — time-to-match, call setup, and second-room-within-7-days must be measured from day one, separately, or every diagnosis will be wrong.

What we do not do: add features to fix a retention problem. Features are what Clubhouse added on the way down.
