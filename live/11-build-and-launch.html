1. Self-hosted ASR: when to switch, what to buy, how not to break the recap

1.1 The premise correction

"Route audio to our own GPUs without degrading latency" assumes audio is transcribed live. It is not, and that is the entire cost model. Audio is captured on-device, VAD-gated, and uploaded after the room ends as a 6-minute window plus a 5% sample. There is no live transcription path in v1, so there is no live latency to degrade.

The two latency budgets that do exist:

Path Budget Why
Recap appears after room end P90 ≤ 20s (upload 3s + ASR 7s + LLM 6s + render 4s) The user is reading a summary, not waiting on a call
Report clip upload P90 ≤ 1.5s The user is upset; the clip must leave the device before they change their mind

The 1.5s target belongs to the report, not the recap. Anyone who puts live captions in the v1 scope has re-created the $0.35/room cost trap with a nicer name.

1.2 The flip threshold

Value
Volume trigger 2.5M speaker-minutes/month sustained for 4 weeks (~65k MAU at 38.9 min/MAU)
Cost trigger API ASR spend > $14.5k/month for 2 consecutive months
Build trigger 1.2M speaker-minutes/month (~30k MAU) — start the shadow deployment here
Payback $150k build ÷ ($14.5k − $2.8k monthly saving) ≈ 13 months at the trigger point

Below 2.5M minutes the API is cheaper once you charge the build to the P&L. Above it, self-hosting wins on every month thereafter. The mistake to avoid is building at 10k MAU because "it will be cheaper at scale" — it will be, and it will also be a $150k distraction while the product is unproven.

1.3 Hardware

Component Spec Why
GPU NVIDIA L4, 24 GB (g6.xlarge, 4 vCPU, 16 GB RAM) Best price/perf for Whisper inference; FP8 support; $0.805/hr on-demand, ~$0.35 spot
Minimum cluster 3 GPUs across 2 AZs (1 warm spare) One AZ failure must not stop recaps
At 200k MAU 8 + 2 spare = 10 ~7.8M speaker-min/month
At 400k MAU 16 + 2 spare = 18 ~15.5M speaker-min/month
Model distil-whisper-large-v3 via CTranslate2, int8; large-v3 for segments with confidence below threshold ~6× faster than large-v3 at ~1–2 WER points worse on clean audio
Batching 8 segments, 30s chunks, VAD-segmented Fills the GPU without padding silence
Throughput ~40× realtime per L4 at batch 8 1.0M minutes/month at 60% utilisation
Orchestration EKS + Karpenter; 3 on-demand base, spot for burst Spot interruptions drain the queue, not the request
Queue SQS, visibility 300s, DLQ at 3 attempts Recap is async; retries are free
Storage S3 (audio, 7-day lifecycle), Postgres (transcripts), no audio in the DB Cheapest correct split

Why not A10G: the L4 is ~15% slower per token but ~25% cheaper per hour and supports FP8. At this volume that is $2–3k/month. Why not a single big A100: utilisation would sit below 20% at launch, and a single GPU is a single point of failure for every recap in the product.

1.4 Migration path

Phase When What happens Exit criterion
0 — Build 30k MAU Queue, worker, model serving, S3 lifecycle, dashboards 1,000 test minutes processed
1 — Shadow 30–65k MAU 5% of rooms transcribed by both API and self-hosted; compare WER, cost, p95 job time WER delta < 1.5 points on a 500-room eval set, for 4 weeks
2 — Ramp At trigger 5% → 25% → 100% by hash(room_id), 2 weeks per step No regression in recap quality score (thumbs, not stars)
3 — Fallback 30 days after 100% API kept as cold fallback; one flag flips a room back Zero recap failures above 0.5%

Quality gate, not just cost: the recap is the paid artifact. A 2-point WER regression on accented English is worth more than the $77k/month saved, so the eval set is 500 rooms stratified by accent (US, Mexican Spanish-influenced, Indian English, Nigerian English) and topic. If distil fails one cohort, route that cohort to large-v3 on the same GPUs.

1.5 The one thing that will go wrong

Queue backlog at peak. Rooms end in bursts at :00 and :15. 400 concurrent rooms ending within two minutes is 400 jobs × 6 minutes of audio. At 40× realtime and 18 GPUs, that clears in ~3.5 minutes — acceptable, but only if autoscaling is warm. Pre-scale on a schedule at 19:00–23:00 local, and set the recap copy for a slow night: "Your recap is still being written — it'll be here in a minute." No spinner.

2. The Austin playbook

2.1 The $50k problem

The brief says $50k for launch. Run the CAC test on it before spending a dollar:

Value
Target at D90 2,000 DAU ≈ 5,000 MAU
Payers at 6.5% 325
LTV per payer (10 §5.5) $93
Cohort LTV $30,225
Spend $50,000 LTV/CAC = 0.6× ❌
Spend $14,000 2.2× ⚠️
Spend $10,000 3.0× ✅

Spending the full $50k to reach 2,000 DAU destroys value. It is a 4× overspend against the cohort it buys. The 90-day plan therefore spends $10–14k on variable acquisition and holds the rest.

CAC at launch is ~$8–9, not $3.50. That is honest and it is fine: the constraint applies at steady state, when referral and organic dominate. The model amendment: Y1 blended CAC $9, not $3.50 → Y1 net moves from −$1.24M to −$1.46M. Y2 and Y3 are unchanged because they are referral-led.

Anyone who reports $3.50 CAC in month one is dividing total spend by signups they did not pay for.

2.2 Week 0–4: the first 120 speakers

Where they come from, in order of yield:

Source Target Why it works

The ask (exact copy, LinkedIn or in person):

We're building a small live-audio room for AI and philosophy in Austin. Fifteen minutes, four people, no audience, no recording, no DMs afterwards.

We're inviting 120 people to be Founding Speakers before we open to everyone. You pick a night; we fill the other three seats with people who actually want to talk about your topic.

Three months of Pro, free. Two sessions a week for eight weeks — four hours total. Interested?

The incentive is status, access, and a small obligation — not cash. Paying speakers creates a two-class room and a perverse incentive to fill silence. Founding Speakers get: Pro free, a title on their profile-less profile (a line on the recap only), topic veto, and a monthly dinner with the other speakers.

Screening: a 10-minute call. Can they hold a conversation on one topic for fifteen minutes without dominating? If they talk for eight of the ten minutes, they are not a speaker, they are a risk.

Week 0–4 targets: 120 confirmed, 60% show rate on any given night, 3 nights/week (Mon AI, Wed Philosophy, Thu Tech careers), 50 completed rooms.

2.4 Week 9–12: listeners into speakers

The 5:1 ratio means 600 speakers behind 3,000 participants. Speakers are the scarce side; listeners convert by being asked once, at the right moment.

Trigger Ask Conversion expectation
After a user's second completed room Recap line: "Want to lead Thursday's AI room? One tap." 8–12% tap
After a third room in the same topic "You've been in three AI rooms. Take a seat on the roster." 15%
After a recap they shared "That was a good one. Lead one?" 20%
In-room, at 12:00 Nothing. Never ask mid-conversation —

Speaker onboarding is 15 minutes, not an application: a prompt card for their topic, a mic check, the three rules (one question at a time, yield after 90 seconds, no recording), and a human on call for their first session.

Week 9–12 targets: 600 speakers, 2,000 DAU, P90 ≤ 30s, 5.5% conversion, Pro ≥ 30% of new subscriptions.

Five taps, one phone number. The OS microphone permission is the only system dialog, and it appears at the moment of joining, not at launch.

Verification without friction: phone OTP before room 1 (accountability), liveness before room 3 (after value). No name, no photo, no email, no location, no goals form. Display name is assigned (Alex · B1) and editable in Settings.

3.2 The first-room guarantee

A new user's first room must be full, on-topic, and at peak. Three mechanisms, in order:

  1. Trusted-only pairing for rooms 1–2. A new account is only placed in a room that already contains at least one speaker with 10+ completed rooms and zero upheld reports. If none is available, the user waits — up to 90 seconds.
  2. Speaker ping. At 60 seconds of waiting, rostered speakers for tonight's topic who are online get one silent in-app line: "A new person is waiting for the AI room." One tap to take the seat, +1 match priority for the night.
  3. AI third seat. At 90 seconds, if two people are waiting, the AI co-host takes the third seat for up to 3 minutes (D4). It is labelled. No fake humans, ever.

What we do not do: pad the room with bots that pretend to be people, seed fake "4 talking" counts, or hold a new user for a "better" match beyond 90 seconds. The first room is the product promise; faking it is how the promise dies.

3.3 The mid-room limit

The user crosses 30 minutes inside a room. Nothing happens. No toast, no colour change, no timer warning. The room ends at 15:00 like every other room.

Recap copy, exact:

Session complete · 15:00

Three things you said
  1. …
  2. …
  3. …

Words you reached for   14
New phrase              stakeholder

One filled button (Done). One text link. No modal, no red, no countdown. Tapping the link opens the paywall sheet; tapping Done goes Home, where the only change is a single muted line under the room card.

The next Join is the paywall. Not the recap, not the app open. The user is told the fact once, calmly, and then given a choice at the moment they act.

4. Safety engineering

4.1 Vibe Check: removing a person without ending the room

Client A ── vibe_check(target=B) ──┐
Client C ── vibe_check(target=B) ──┤→ Redis: ZADD vibe:{room}:{B} score member=A|C
                                   │   (score = reporter weight, TTL 20 min)
                                   ▼
                        weight ≥ 2.0 ?
                                   │ yes
                                   ▼
             LiveKit RemoveParticipant(room, B)   ← server-side, 40ms
             revoke B's token · unpublish B's tracks
                                   │
                                   ▼
        Room A/C continue. No renegotiation, no gap, no notification to B
        beyond "You left the room."
Detail Implementation
Reporter weight 1.0 if in the room > 3 minutes and has spoken; 0.5 if joined < 60s ago; 0 if flagged for prior false reports
Threshold 2.0 — two normal reporters, or one normal and two drive-bys
Window 20 minutes, cleared when the room ends
Rate limit 3 Vibe Checks per user per day; 10 per month; abuse review at 5
What B sees "You left the room." No accusation, no reporter identity, no room re-entry for 24h
What A/C see The circle greys and disappears. No banner, no confirmation, no "thanks for reporting"
Score Unchanged. A vibe_check event is logged; only an upheld report moves the score
Appeal Any access restriction beyond the room triggers a review; the restricted user gets one screen with the reason and an Appeal button, 72h SLA

Why server-side removal: the client cannot be trusted to drop a participant, and a client-side "mute" leaves the offender's audio flowing. RemoveParticipant is atomic and works even if the offender's app is unresponsive.

The failure mode to design for: two friends removing a third. The weight system and the 3-minute speaking requirement raise the cost, but do not eliminate it. That is why the room action is instant and the account action is not — one is reversible, the other is not.

4.2 The 60-second buffer

Stage Implementation
Capture AVAudioEngine tap on the output mix (local mic + remote tracks summed), so the clip contains what the reporter heard — including the offender
Codec Opus, 24 kbps, 20ms frames → ~180 KB for 60s
Storage Memory only. AES-GCM with a session key in iOS Keychain / Android Keystore. Never written to disk
Ring 60s circular buffer, overwritten continuously
On report Prompt: "Attach the last 30 seconds? It helps us review." Default on, one tap to decline
If yes Decrypt → presigned S3 PUT → zero the buffer → server stores 30 days → access-logged
If no Zero the buffer immediately. Nothing uploaded
If force-quit / OS kill Buffer is gone. Nothing persisted, nothing recoverable
If backgrounded > 30s iOS suspends audio; the buffer contains a gap. The clip is marked has_gap=true; reviewers see a waveform with the gap marked
Retention Audio 30 days → deleted; transcript kept 24 months; case metadata 24 months

The honest limitation, stated to the reviewer and in the policy: the clip is the reporter's device view. If the offender spoke while the reporter was muted, or the report is filed after the app was killed, there is no clip. The case then rests on the 5% sample, the score history, and prior reports. A report without a clip is still investigated — it is just slower.

Disclosure, one line at room start, no modal: "If you report someone, the last 30 seconds can be attached. Nothing is recorded otherwise."

6. Infrastructure cost: API vs GPU

Basis: 38.9 speaker-minutes per MAU per month (5.4 rooms × 7.2 minutes). GPU = L4 at $0.805/hr, 1.0M minutes/GPU/month at 60% utilisation, plus 2 spare. API = $0.0058/speaker-minute.

Where the flip pays: at 50k MAU the saving is $7.7k/month against a $12.5k/month build amortisation — negative until month 13. At 200k it is $37.8k/month, which pays the build back in four months. The trigger in §1.2 (2.5M minutes, ~65k MAU) sits between those points and is the conservative choice.

Reconciliation to the model: 10 §5.2 carries $0.40/MAU at 400k. This table is $0.11 of infrastructure. The gap is moderation ($0.14), support and verification ($0.07), and an $0.08 peak reserve for egress variance, retries, and the nightly burst. The reserve is not padding — it is the difference between a model that survives a bad Tuesday and one that does not.

7. The hard stop

7.1 Trigger

D90: DAU < 800 AND D7 < 20%. Both, not either. DAU alone can be a marketing miss; D7 alone can be a small-cohort artifact. Together they mean the group format has not found a habit.

7.2 The runbook

Day Action Owner
0 Freeze all acquisition spend and ambassador hiring. Post in #launch: "Hard stop triggered, decision in 10 days." Growth
0 Snapshot the dashboard, export the cohort data, cancel the next two prime-topic nights Eng
1 20 user interviews: 10 churned, 10 retained. One question: "What did you expect that you did not get?" Product
2 Cohort analysis: retention by first-room type (AI vs Philosophy), by speaker presence, by time-to-room Data
3 Cost teardown: actual COGS per room vs $0.068; actual moderation hours per 1k rooms Finance
5 Written recommendation to the board: pivot, narrow, or stop. One page, three options, one recommendation Founders
7 Board decision. If pivot: 30-day plan. If stop: wind-down plan Board
10 Communicate to the team and to users (if stopping, 30 days' notice) Founders

What we do not do in the 10 days: ship features, run a discount, hire, or announce anything publicly. Panic-shipping during a hard stop is how a 10-day decision becomes a 6-month one.

7.3 The pivot: B2B2C cohorts

The honest read of a D90 miss is usually density, not demand: the product works when a room is full, and a consumer app cannot guarantee a full room at 2,000 DAU. A buyer who supplies the density fixes the only broken variable.

The pitch (exact copy, to a bootcamp or university career centre):

Your students can pass the technical screen and still fail the interview, because they have never said the answer out loud to another person.

Eloq is a live-audio room: four students, fifteen minutes, one interview question, an AI recap with three fixes. No video, no recording, no DMs.

We run a four-week pilot with 24 of your students. Two sessions a week, 20:00, facilitated by our Founding Speakers. You get completion and confidence data; we get a reference cohort.

$2,500 for the pilot. If completion is above 70%, we talk about a per-seat licence.

Element Detail
Buyer Bootcamp career services, university career centre, corporate L&D for non-native hires
Price $2,500 / 4-week pilot / 24 seats; $49/seat/month after
What we supply Rooms, speakers, schedule, recap, completion reporting
What they supply 24 motivated students and a 20:00 slot
Why it works Density is bought, not manufactured; the buyer already has the cohort
Why it might not Sales cycle, procurement, and a product that becomes a service business with 40% margins

Decision rule: if three of five pilot conversations convert to a paid pilot within 30 days, pivot. If not, the problem is the product, and a B2B wrapper will not save it.

7.4 The stop

If neither the consumer product nor the cohort pilot shows demand at D120: stop matching, notify users 30 days out, export their data, delete all audio within 7 days, and return remaining capital. The one thing we do not do is keep a small team running a quiet app to avoid admitting the answer.
