1. The decision log

D1 — Free cap: 30 min/day and 90 min/week
Decision Both limits, whichever binds first. Shown as a 4px hairline, not a counter.
Why The average free MAU uses 5.4 rooms/month (81 minutes), so the daily cap binds for under 6% of users. The weekly cap is what contains the tail: a maxed free user costs $0.94/month at 90 min/week versus $2.60/month at 30 min/day uncapped.
Cost Average free MAU $0.61/month; maxed free user $0.94; the tail is bounded, which is what makes the free tier forecastable.
Why not 20/day It cuts average usage by ~4% (saves $0.02/MAU) and costs the headline. It is a worse trade: the cap that matters is weekly, and 20/day reads stingy in ASO copy.
Reversal trigger Free-tier COGS > $0.70/MAU/month for two consecutive months → drop to 20/day and 60/week.

D2 — Pro pricing: $19.99 at launch; test $24.99 at month 6
Decision $19.99/mo · $199/yr at launch. A $24.99 test runs only after the practice record and voice feedback ship.
Why $24.99 without an artifact is a price with no proof. At $19.99 and a 35% Pro mix, net ARPP is $9.52 and break-even conversion is 4.2% (§5). Moving to $24.99 before the record exists risks a 20–30% conversion drop to protect ~$0.60 of ARPP.
Why the mix matters more than the price Plus alone breaks even at 6.7%; a 35% Pro mix at 4.2%. The Pro tier is the business, which is why the interview rooms, the record, and voice feedback sit there and not in Plus.
Reversal trigger Pro share < 25% of new subscriptions at month 6 → hold $19.99, add annual-only discount, and stop building Pro features until the mix moves.

D3 — Vibe Check: drop the room instantly; move the score only after review
Decision Two reports remove the reported user from that room, immediately, for everyone's safety. The account is untouched until a human reviews.
Why Two people in a five-person room is 40% of the room and can be coordinated. Letting two reports move a score is a brigading tool with a safety label. The room drop is reversible (they join another room in 30 seconds); a score collapse is not.
What the reporter sees The person's circle goes quiet and greys out; the room continues. One line: "They've left." No accusation, no confirmation of who reported.
Appeal Any access restriction beyond the room requires review. The restricted user gets one screen, one reason, one Appeal button, and a 72-hour human decision.
Reversal trigger 30-day repeat-offender rate > 5% of reported users → add a 24-hour protective exclusion before review (matching scope only, never the score, always appealable).

D4 — AI co-host: paid rooms only, plus a capped free rescue
Decision AI participates in (a) paid Topic Boosts, (b) Pro Seed Rooms, (c) a free stall rescue: after 60 seconds with two people waiting, max 3 minutes, max once per user per day.
Why An AI participant costs $0.03–0.06/minute → $0.45–0.90 per 15-minute room, 7–13× an entire human room. A free co-host in 20% of rooms adds ~$0.25/MAU and cuts Y3 gross margin by ~6 points; break-even conversion rises 1.6 points.
Why the rescue exists Without it, two people who almost matched leave. Three minutes of AI costs $0.09–0.18 — the cheapest retention spend in the product.
Reversal trigger P90 time-to-room > 45s for four consecutive weeks → extend the free rescue to 5 minutes, but only in the two lowest-density topics, never globally.

D5 — Room length: 15 minutes for topic rooms, 10 for Practice
Decision AI / Science / Philosophy / Tech / Career = 15:00. Language and interview drills = 10:00.
Why A topic needs 15 minutes to get past positions and into reasons. A drill does not: interview and language rooms have a fixed structure (question → answer → feedback) that degrades into filler after ten.
Cost $0.068 per 15-minute room versus $0.045 per 10-minute room. Practice rooms are 34% cheaper per session and higher frequency — they subsidise the topic rooms' cost.
Reversal trigger Topic-room completion < 70% at day 60 → cut to 12 minutes. Completion > 88% with strong recap engagement at day 90 → test 18.

2. The sampled ASR architecture

2.1 Why the pipeline is the business

A 15-minute room with four speakers is 60 participant-minutes but only about 18 speaker-minutes of actual speech — people listen most of the time. Transcribing the stream blindly costs 3.3× what the conversation contains. Transcribing the whole conversation costs 5.5× what the product needs.

2.2 On-device VAD

Parameter Value Why
Model Silero VAD v5, ONNX, ~1.8 MB Runs in <1% CPU on a 2020 phone; no vendor dependency
Frame 30 ms, 16 kHz mono Standard; matches Opus frame size
Threshold 0.5, hysteresis 0.35 Prevents flicker at the edge of speech
Min speech 250 ms Rejects coughs and door slams
Hangover 300 ms Keeps the tail of a word; ASR accuracy drops sharply on clipped endings
Output [{start_ms, end_ms, energy}] per speaker ~2 KB per room. This is the only thing uploaded by default

What it saves: silence, listening, and background noise. In a healthy four-person room that is ~70% of participant-minutes. It also produces the turn-taking data the balance meter and the score need — for free, before any server sees audio.

What it costs: aggressive VAD misses whispers and quiet speakers, which biases the transcript toward confident voices. Hangover plus a 0.35 floor recovers most of it. Any room where VAD speech share is < 20% is flagged for a human listen — that pattern is either a broken mic or someone being talked over.

2.3 Selecting the 6-minute recap window

Not the first six minutes, and not the loudest six. The first six are introductions; the loudest six are arguments without conclusions. The recap needs the arc: what was asked, where it turned, how it landed.

Algorithm — four 90-second blocks, dynamic programming over 30-second buckets:

  1. Score each 30s bucket: 0.40 × speech_density + 0.25 × turn_count_normalised + 0.20 × recency + 0.15 × novelty(energy variance).
  2. Subtract 0.5 × overlap_ratio for crosstalk — unintelligible audio is worse than quiet audio.
  3. Pick four 90s blocks, maximising total score, with constraints: at least one block from 0:30–3:30, at least one from the final 3:00, at most two adjacent.
  4. Concatenate, transcribe, summarise with timestamps so the recap can cite "at 11:20, someone said…".

Why contiguous blocks: sentence-level stitching produces summaries that read like a random sample of quotes. Blocks preserve the argument. Four 90-second pieces are enough for three takeaways and one 30-second highlight.

Fallback: if the room is < 6 minutes or has < 4 minutes of speech, transcribe everything and label the recap "short session."

2.4 The 5% sample
Rate 5% of rooms, whole-room (not 5% of each room) — you need context to judge a room
Selection HMAC(server_secret, room_id + date) → uniform, unpredictable to the client, reproducible in an audit
Stratification Forced minimums: 2% of each topic, 100% of rooms with an upheld report in the last 90 days, 100% of rooms where a participant's score changed
Use Calibration, P1 discovery, and score-model training. Never automatic enforcement
Cost 0.9 speaker-minutes per room at 5% of rooms → $0.005/room
Retention 30 days, then deleted; transcripts kept, audio deleted at 7 days

The honest limit: 5% sampling will miss most harassment. It is a backstop for pattern discovery, not a detector. The primary signal is the Vibe Check; the second is the report buffer. Any document that claims otherwise is selling.

2.5 The 60-second moderation buffer

The server is not recording, so the buffer lives on the reporter's device:

Stage Behaviour
During the room Encrypted roll of the last 60s in memory, keyed per session, never written to disk by default
On Report The call drops first (§09). Then: "Attach the last 30 seconds? It helps us review." Default on, one tap to decline
Upload The 30s clip is uploaded with the report, encrypted in transit, access-logged
If declined / app killed No clip. The case proceeds on the sampled audio, the score history, and prior reports
Retention Audio 30 days, then deleted; case metadata 24 months

Why the reporter's device, not the server: server-side continuous recording of every room is a privacy liability, a breach target, and the exact architecture that makes the ASR cost unaffordable. The buffer is a feature of the report, not of the product.

Disclosure, one line, at room start: "If you report someone, the last 30 seconds can be attached. Nothing is recorded otherwise." That sentence is the whole privacy policy for audio.

3. Liquidity: the only number that decides this product

3.1 The maths, stated plainly

Filling a room needs three arrivals in a topic. With N people concurrently in that topic and 15-minute sessions, arrivals arrive at λ = N/15 per minute.

  • Mean wait for three: W = 45/N minutes.
  • P90 is roughly 2.3 × the mean (Poisson arrivals).
Concurrent in the prime topic Mean fill P90 fill
30 1.5 min 3.5 min
60 45 s 1.7 min
90 30 s 70 s
150 18 s 42 s
210 13 s 30 s
300 9 s 21 s

To guarantee a room in 30 seconds at P90, you need ~210 concurrent users in that topic. At 12% peak concurrency, that is ~1,750 DAU — call it 2,000 with topic skew.

DAU in one metro Peak concurrent (12%) P90 fill, one topic
800 96 65 s
1,500 180 35 s
2,000 240 26 s ✅
4,000 480 13 s (two topics simultaneously)

3.2 The unlock: schedule the topic, not the person

The brief asks for a 4-person room on "AI or Philosophy" within 30 seconds. Two topics at 30s P90 needs ~4,000 DAU. One topic needs 2,000. So:

Launch with one prime topic per evening. Monday AI, Tuesday Philosophy, Wednesday Tech Careers, Thursday Science, Friday open. The Home screen shows one room; the topic chips reflect tonight's prime topic plus whatever else has density. This concentrates demand by ~5× and makes the specific-topic promise true at a fifth of the users.

This is the single most important growth decision in the plan. It is also the least glamorous: a schedule is not a feature.

3.3 The 90-day playbook — Austin first

Why Austin: UT Austin + ACC + a dense early-career tech population, one time zone, English-first (no language-pair liquidity problem), and a startup community that will tolerate a rough beta.

Phase Days Supply Demand Ships Gate to next
0 — Seed −14 → 0 120 founding speakers (3 nights/week), 3 campus clubs 400 waitlist Prime-topic schedule, stall rescue, Vibe Check, report flow 50 test rooms, 0 P1 defects
1 — Alpha 1–30 120 → 250 speakers 600 activated users AI + Philosophy nights, 2 ambassadors D30: 300 activated, P90 ≤90s, D7 ≥25%
2 — Density 31–60 400 speakers 1,200 DAU Daily prime topic, recap, Plus D60: 700 DAU, P90 ≤45s, conversion ≥4%
3 — Proof 61–90 600 speakers 2,000 DAU Pro, Career rooms, second campus (Rice/Houston) D90: P90 ≤30s, D30 ≥15%, conversion ≥5.5%

The supply ratio: at 2,000 DAU you need 600 rostered speakers (30%) because half show up on a given night and half of those are online at 8pm. That is the number that makes the queue feel alive; it is also the number that makes the unit economics look worse, because speakers are users, not inventory.

Three rules that keep the seed honest: 1. No paid presence. Speakers get Pro free and a title, not money. 2. No fake rooms. An AI co-host is labelled, and never the majority. 3. Publish the real P90 fill time on Home during alpha ("usually under 2 minutes"). Trust is the launch asset.

3.4 The AI seed fallback, and its kill signal

The AI co-host is a third seat, not a host: it asks one question, yields, and leaves the moment a third human joins. Rules:

  • Enters only after 60s with two people in a room, or in a paid Boost.
  • Maximum 3 minutes free (D4), 15 in a Boost.
  • Speaks ≤ 40% of the airtime in any 60-second window.
  • Named "Ana · AI" on the circle; never claims otherwise.
  • If a room has an AI for its entire duration, it is not counted as a "human room" in the North Star metric.

Kill signal for the fallback: if > 30% of room-minutes involve an AI participant for two consecutive weeks, the fallback is no longer a fallback. Action: cut free rescue to boosts only, drop to two topics, and re-run the density plan. If it stays above 30% after that, the product is a chatbot and should be priced like one.

4. Paywall and conversion

4.1 Trial: 7 days, annual only

Plan Trial Why
Annual $79.99 7 days The trial exists to prove the habit, not to discount a month
Monthly $9.99 None A 7-day trial on a monthly plan is a churn machine: 55% cancel on day 0 and the rest were never going to pay

Trial rules: starts at purchase, not at install. Full access. Reminder at day 5 (one line), day 6 (one line), no countdown. Cancel is one tap in Settings, and the app says so on the sheet.

4.2 The mid-room limit

The user hits 30 minutes during a room. Nothing happens.

Moment UI
5 minutes past the limit Nothing. The hairline stays 1px. No toast, no colour
Room ends at 15:00 Normal ending, normal chime
Recap One quiet line under the facts: "That was your last room on Free today."
Next "Join" tap The sheet (§5 of 09)
Next app open Home shows the limit line and one button: Plus

Why no interruption: a paywall mid-conversation is the single fastest way to make a user feel ambushed, and it teaches them to fear the timer. The limit is a fact about tomorrow, not a cliff in the middle of today.

4.3 The day-0 problem: activation before the ask

55% of trial cancellations happen on day 0 because the user pays before they have had a good room. The fix is not a better paywall; it is an onboarding grant:

Step What happens Why
Install → first room No cap for the first 3 rooms or 48 hours, whichever comes first The free tier is 90 min/week; the grant guarantees three complete rooms before any limit is visible
Room 1 Matched into a prime-topic room at peak, never a niche one The first room must be full; density is a product feature
Room 2 Same night or next, one tap from the recap Habit forms on the second room, not the first
Room 3 Recap shows one quiet line: "Plus keeps this going." No sheet The ask appears only when a limit is hit
Day 3+ Normal 90 min/week applies By now the user has a reason, or they were never going to convert

The trial offer appears in exactly one place: the paywall sheet, after a blocked action. Never on open, never on the recap, never as an interstitial. A user who never hits a limit may never see a price — that is correct.

The numbers this is designed to move: install → room 1 ≥ 70%; room 1 → room 2 ≥ 45% within 7 days; trial start ≥ 8% of users who see the sheet; day-0 trial cancel < 35% (versus the 55% baseline).

5.4 The direct answer on 40%

Y3 gross margin lands at 39%, not 15% — but the 15% was a v5 arithmetic choice, not a sampling failure.

Three things are true at once:

  1. Sampled ASR is already inside the 15% number. Locking it in protects the model from a 5× cost error; it does not add margin.
  2. The jump to 39% is bought with vertical integration — self-hosted ASR at 400k MAU. If the company stays on API ASR, Y3 gross margin is ~20%, and the business never clears its fixed costs.
  3. 39% is not 60%. Voice AI with a free tier is an infrastructure business. The only routes to 55%+ are a higher Pro mix, usage-based pricing, or cutting the free tier — in that order.

Break-even conversion:

Y1 Y2 Y3
Cost / MAU $0.61 $0.51 $0.40
Net ARPP $9.35 $9.63 $9.52
Break-even conversion 6.5% 5.3% 4.2%
Conversion at plan 4.2% 5.8% 7.0%
Margin at plan negative +9% +39%

The brief's 3–5% target: still below break-even in Y1 and Y2, viable only in Y3. At 3% in Y3 the product loses $0.11/MAU/month — $550k/year at 400k MAU. The plan does not survive a 3% conversion rate at any point.

5.5 The CAC correction: the v5 ratio was measured on the wrong denominator

The v5 model reported LTV/CAC of 3.1×. That was LTV per MAU ÷ CAC per MAU, which is not a business metric. Subscription economics need CAC per paying customer:

CAC per payer = CAC per activated user ÷ conversion rate

Conversion CAC per activation CAC per payer LTV LTV / CAC
3% $3.50 $117 $93 0.8× ❌
5% $3.50 $70 $93 1.3× ❌
7% $3.50 $50 $93 1.9× ⚠️
10% $3.50 $35 $93 2.7× ✅
7% $12.00 $171 $93 0.5× ❌

What this means, without softening it:

  1. Paid social at $12–16 per install is fatal. At a 7% conversion it implies a $171–229 CAC against a $93 LTV. Every dollar of it destroys value.
  2. The only viable acquisition is campus- and community-led at ≤ $3.50 per activated user. That is what the ambassador model in 08 §6.3 was priced at, and it is now a hard constraint, not a preference.
  3. The plan's 7% conversion is enough only because CAC is low. If CAC drifts to $6, LTV/CAC falls to 1.1× and the company cannot grow profitably at any scale.
  4. The model in §5.3 uses $3.50 per activation for exactly this reason. The acquisition line is $140k / $630k / $1.82M, not a marketing budget that can be scaled at will.

The consequence for growth: this product cannot be bought. It has to be seeded campus by campus, which caps growth at roughly 1.5–2× per quarter and makes the 90-day density plan the growth plan. If the board wants 3× per year, the answer is a different conversion rate, not a bigger ad budget.

Operating break-even arrives in year four at ~1.2–1.5M MAU, on ~$12M of revenue and a 47% gross margin. Total capital required: $7.5–8M, not $3M. Anyone pitching this as a two-year path to profitability is mispricing ASR and moderation.

If that capital is not available, the honest alternatives are: (a) cut the free tier to 15 min/week and target 8% conversion, (b) price Plus at $14.99 and accept a smaller funnel, or (c) drop topic rooms and return to 1:1 language practice, where cost per session is half. There is no fourth option that keeps the product and the price.

6. Kill signals

Reviewed at day 30, 60, and 90. Each has an owner and a pre-committed action. Pivot means change the format; stop means stop.

Day 30 — "Is the room worth 15 minutes?"

Metric Threshold If missed
Activated users (≥1 completed room) ≥ 300 Extend alpha 30 days; do not add features
P90 time-to-room ≤ 90s Add a second prime topic night; recruit 100 more speakers
Rooms per activated user ≥ 1.2 The first room is not good enough; rewrite prompts before anything else
D7 retention ≥ 25% Pivot: move to scheduled tables at fixed times
Report rate ≤ 3.0 / 1k room-min Freeze growth; audit matching and score
AI share of room-minutes ≤ 25% The network is not forming; cut the free rescue
Zero confirmed minor incidents 0 STOP. Full stop, external audit

Day 60 — "Does it retain without a schedule?"

Metric Threshold If missed
DAU ≥ 700 Density plan is failing; concentrate to one campus
P90 time-to-room ≤ 45s Drop to three topics
D7 / D30 ≥ 30% / ≥ 15% Pivot to cohort-based rooms (bootcamp, club)
Conversion (any paid) ≥ 4% Reprice Plus to $11.99 before touching the free tier
Pro share of new subs ≥ 25% Career rooms are not landing; stop building them
Gross margin per cohort > −$0.10/MAU The unit economics are not converging; cut free AI first

Day 90 — "Does it scale, or does it only work in Austin?"

Metric Threshold If missed
DAU ≥ 2,000 Second campus is not viable; stay single-metro and cut costs
P90 time-to-room ≤ 30s Pivot: the "instant" promise is false; go scheduled
D30 retention ≥ 18% Pivot to a cohort product with a fixed weekly time
Conversion ≥ 5.5% STOP paid growth. The product is a utility, not a subscription. Cut free to 20 min/week and re-test at 8%
Pro share ≥ 30% Collapse Pro into Plus, sell the record as a one-off
AI share ≤ 20% Human network is not forming; stop scaling
Gross margin (run-rate) ≥ 30% Reprice or cut the free tier; do not raise again
Confirmed P1 with no prior report 0 Raise sampling in the cohort, publish the change, freeze marketing for 30 days

The two hard stops

  1. Any confirmed minor in an 18+ room. Matching stops immediately. External audit. No relaunch without a new verification vendor and a public report.
  2. Day 90 with DAU < 800 and D7 < 20%. The group format has not found a habit. Stop the consumer product, return remaining capital, or pivot to a cohort/B2B2C model where the buyer supplies the density (bootcamps, universities) rather than the consumer app.

What is not a kill signal: slow revenue in month one, a competitor launching, or a bad press cycle. Those are noise. The numbers above are structure.
