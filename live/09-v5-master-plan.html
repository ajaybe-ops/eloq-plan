Version 5.0 · 2026-11-18 · Status: supersedes the pricing and room model in 05 and 08

Eloq v1–v4 was a language-exchange product. v5 changes the core object: the room is now a topic, not a person. Science, tech, philosophy, AI, careers — three to six people, fifteen minutes, one subject. Language practice and interview prep survive as two room families inside it.

That is a real pivot, not a repositioning, and it changes the economics in both directions: group rooms cost more per session than 1:1s, but they are the only format where "high-signal discussion" is a product rather than a promise.

This document is the monetization and growth plan. It answers what is free, where the paywall sits, how StoreKit and Play Billing are wired, what each minute of audio actually costs, and whether the business scales. It ends with what I think will kill it.

The one-sentence strategy: be the only live-audio app where the room is worth the fifteen minutes, the people are filtered by behaviour rather than vibes, and nobody can message you afterwards.

2. The recommendation engine, described honestly

"Deep learning matches you to productive rooms" is true but unremarkable. Every consumer app has a ranker. What matters is what it optimises and what it refuses to optimise.

2.1 The five-second intent

There is no five-second inference screen. Inference at that speed means either always-on listening or a form — both are wrong. Instead:

  1. One chip on Home: AI · Science · Philosophy · Tech careers · Practice English. That is the intent, in one tap.
  2. A stored profile built from the topics of rooms the user completed, embedded offline. No raw audio is kept.
  3. A decayed prior so last month's interest does not own this month's recommendations.

Five seconds is the budget for the tap, not for a model to guess. Guessing wrong is worse than asking once.

2.2 The ranking stack

Layer What it does Model Latency budget
Candidate retrieval 200 rooms → 40 Two-tower embeddings (topic text + user history), ANN index 15 ms
Ranker 40 → 6 Gradient-boosted trees on 30 features 10 ms
Exploration 1 of 6 slots Thompson sampling per topic cluster 1 ms
Safety filter Remove excluded pairs, minors, quarantined accounts Rules, evaluated first 1 ms

Features that matter: topic match, language fit, room age, seat count, expected speaking time for this user, partner completion rate, prior room overlap, time-of-day cohort.

Features that must not exist: gender, nationality, accent, inferred age beyond the 18+ gate, photo, follower count, spend. A ranker that learns "this user joins rooms with women" is a harassment engine with a loss function.

2.3 The Productivity Score

The brief asks for a hidden score from speaking ratio, sentiment, and report frequency. Two of those three are traps:

  • Speaking-to-listening ratio punishes quiet people, non-native speakers, and anyone in a room with a dominant speaker. It measures extroversion, not value.
  • Sentiment analysis of speech is unreliable across accents and cultures, and "negative sentiment" is often just disagreement — which is the point of a philosophy room.
  • Report frequency is gameable in both directions: brigading inflates it, fear suppresses it.

What the score actually uses:

Signal Weight Why it is defensible
Room completion (stayed to 15:00) 30% The cleanest proxy for "this was worth the time"
7-day return after a room 25% Value remembered beats value rated
Turns taken vs. airtime share 15% Rewards participation, not domination
Upheld reports (not raw reports) 20% Only adjudicated harm moves the score
On-topic semantic score 10% Measured on the sampled transcript, not continuously

Range 0–100, starting at 60, 30-day half-life, ±1.5× matching multiplier. It never sets price, never appears publicly, never gates the free tier.

Three guardrails that are non-negotiable:

  1. No automated decision with a significant effect without human review. An access restriction longer than 7 days requires a person. (GDPR Art. 22; also basic fairness.)
  2. Explainability on request: "Your access to Expert rooms is paused — two upheld reports in 30 days. Appeal." One screen, one button.
  3. Brigading resistance: two Vibe Checks drop the room immediately, but only adjudicated reports move the score. Otherwise two friends can delete a third person's account.

2.4 The cold-start fix, and its limit

At low density the backend generates 3–5 seed topics from trending sources and opens them as rooms with an AI co-host in the third seat — not a host on stage. The AI asks one question at a time, yields, and leaves the moment a third human joins.

The limit, stated plainly: a seed room makes the app look alive; it does not make the network alive. If more than 35% of room-minutes have an AI participant, the product is a podcast with a chat box. The gate is in §9.

The asymmetry that matters: C is convertible — a time-waster who finds one good room becomes D. B is contained — the design goal is not reform, it is removing the surface. Treating both the same is how products become either cruel or unsafe.

On "shadow-ban": I will not build a silent global ban. It protects the one person who complained and feeds the offender to the next room. The quarantine above is disclosed, bounded, and reviewed — functionally isolating, legally defensible, and it does not require lying to the user.

5.2 Why 30 minutes a day, and 90 a week

The daily cap sounds generous and is. The weekly cap is the one that controls cost, because the expensive user is not the person who does 30 minutes on Tuesday — it is the person who does 30 minutes every day. 90 minutes/week ≈ six rooms, which is exactly the "three sessions a week" habit the product wants, doubled for enthusiasm.

Both limits are shown in the same place: a 4px hairline under the room count on Home. No counter, no percentage, no guilt copy.

5.4 Micro-transactions: the maths that makes them honest

Item Price Store fee Net Cost to serve Margin

The Boost is priced against AI inference, not against perceived value. A 15-minute room with an AI participant costs $0.03–0.06 per minute (H) → $0.45–0.90. At $1.99 the margin survives a 30% fee and a fully-attended room. At $0.99 it does not. That is the whole pricing argument.

Two design constraints on Boosts:

  1. The room is public. "Invite up to 5 people" is a private room with a payment attached — that is a DM with extra steps, and it recreates the exact surface v5 exists to remove. The buyer publishes a topic; the first five people in the queue join.
  2. One Boost per user per day. Otherwise it becomes a pay-to-monopolise-the-queue feature for anyone with $20.

5.5 Why $9.99 if $12.99 was the number that worked

$9.99 is an acquisition price. It sits under ChatGPT Plus, matches the consumer mental model, and makes the annual ($79.99) a visible 33% saving. But §6 shows it does not cover a generous free tier on its own. The business is Pro, not Plus. Plus exists to convert; Pro exists to pay for the free tier. That is why the Pro tier gets the interview rooms, the voice feedback, and the host seats — the things a person with an interview in three weeks will pay double for.

The number to watch: share of new subscriptions that choose Pro. Below 25%, the model in §7 does not close.

6. Unit economics

6.1 What a minute actually costs

A 15-minute room with four speakers is 60 speaker-minutes. That is the number that breaks naive cost models.

Line Rate Naive (ASR on everything) Recommended (sampled)
SFU audio relay $0.00012 / participant-min (H) $0.007 $0.007
ASR $0.0058 / speaker-min (H) $0.348 (60 min) $0.042 (7.2 min: 6-min summary window + 5% safety sample)
Scoring / classification LLM $0.0004 / room-min $0.006 $0.006
Summary LLM $0.012 / room $0.012 $0.012
Storage, analytics — $0.001 $0.001
Per 15-min room $0.374 $0.068
Per room-minute $0.025 $0.0045

This is the single most important table in the document. Continuous transcription of a four-person room costs $0.35, which is 5.5× the sampled architecture. At 40 rooms a month, a continuous-ASR user costs $14 — more than the $9.35 net from Plus. The product would lose money on every heavy user while claiming 90% margins.

The architecture that makes the price work:

  • On-device VAD and keyword spotting — free, always on, no audio leaves the phone.
  • A 6-minute transcribed window per room for the recap (highest-energy segment, not the first six minutes).
  • A 5% random safety sample, plus the 60-second rolling buffer uploaded only on report.
  • Batch the scoring, never per-token real-time.

The cost of this choice is honest: proactive detection gets weaker. The Vibe Check becomes the primary signal, with sampling as a backstop. That trade is defensible for adults in public rooms; it would not be for 1:1s with minors, which is one more reason the 18+ gate is absolute.

6.3 Break-even conversion

Revenue per MAU = conversion × net ARPP. Cost per MAU = $0.57.

Net ARPP Break-even conversion Margin at 5% Margin at 7%
$8.49 (all Plus, 15% fee) 6.7% −$0.15 +$0.02
$9.35 (20% Pro mix) 6.1% −$0.10 +$0.08
$10.87 (35% Pro mix) 5.2% −$0.03 +$0.19
$12.40 (50% Pro mix, 15% fee) 4.6% +$0.05 +$0.30

The brief's 3–5% target is at or below break-even. At 3% the product loses $0.29 per MAU per month — $72k/month at 250k MAU. At 5% it is roughly flat. The plan only works at 6%+ with a Pro mix above 30%, or at a materially lower cost per room.

Three levers, in order of leverage:

  1. Pro mix (biggest): moving 20% → 40% Pro adds $0.30/MAU of revenue at the same conversion.
  2. ASR discipline: the difference between 12% and 5% sampling is $0.02/room, ~$0.10/MAU, ~1.8 points of break-even conversion.
  3. Free cap: from 30 min/day to 20 min/day moves the average by less than 5% — most free users never hit the cap. It protects the tail, not the P&L. Anyone who tells you the cap is the main lever is guessing.

6.4 The AI-participant trap

An AI co-host speaking for 15 minutes costs $0.45–0.90 — 7–13× an entire human room. It cannot be part of the free tier, and it cannot be "always available" without a per-room budget.

Placement Cost Who pays Verdict
Free rooms, always available $0.45–0.90/room Nobody Never
Free rooms, after 60s of a 2-person stall, max 5 min $0.15–0.30 Nobody Cap at 1/room/day; watch AI share
Boost room (paid) $0.45–0.90 $1.69 net ✅ The only structurally sound use
Pro Seed Room (2/month) $0.45–0.90 $19.99 ✅ Within margin

If AI participation exceeds 35% of room-minutes, the product is subsidising a chatbot. Gate in §9.

8. Cold-start, for group rooms specifically

Group-first is harder than 1:1, and the earlier plan said so. The arithmetic:

  • A 4-person room consumes four users for 15 minutes to serve one session. A 1:1 consumes two.
  • With N users online and 15-minute sessions, a room forms roughly every N/4 arrivals — but only among users whose topics overlap.
  • Split across five topics, a 300-concurrent network gives ~60 per topic. Enough for a room every few seconds; not enough to guarantee depth in any one of them.
  • Topic skew is worse than headcount. 300 concurrent users spread over 40 niche topics is 7 people per topic. This is why the topic taxonomy is capped at five at launch, with niche topics behind a Boost.

Three mitigations, in order:

  1. Concentrate hours. 18:00–23:00 in one corridor. Same as before, more important now.
  2. Start at three, cap at six, allow joins until 8:00. A room that waits for six never starts. A room that starts at three and accepts late joins fills itself.
  3. AI co-host as the third seat — only for Plus/Pro rooms or paid Boosts, only after 60 seconds of a stall, maximum five minutes. Free rooms wait, or the user opens a Boost.

The trap to avoid: using AI seed rooms to make the app look full at launch. It works for a week, then the first real user arrives, finds four bots, and never returns. AI can be the third participant; it can never be the majority of the room.

9. App Store compliance checklist

9.1 Payments

  • [ ] StoreKit 2 for iOS: Product.products(for:), product.purchase(), Transaction.updates listener at launch, VerificationResult JWS check, AppStore.sync() for Restore.
  • [ ] Google Play Billing Library 7+: queryProductDetailsAsync, launchBillingFlow, acknowledge within 3 days, real-time developer notifications to the backend.
  • [ ] Server-side validation for every transaction; entitlements in our database, never trusted from the client.
  • [ ] One subscription group so Plus ↔ Pro upgrades prorate correctly instead of double-charging.
  • [ ] Consumables (summary, Boost) granted server-side, idempotent on transaction ID, with a refund clawback path.
  • [ ] No external payment links in-app (Guideline 3.1.1). The US external-link entitlement exists but is jurisdiction-specific and not worth building the business on.

9.2 Store economics

  • [ ] Enrol in Apple Small Business Program (15% while prior-year proceeds < $1M). Re-apply annually.
  • [ ] Apple's subscription discount: 15% applies after 12 months of continuous paid service for a given subscriber. Model it; it is worth ~4 points of margin by Y3.
  • [ ] Google Play: 15% on subscriptions; 15% on the first $1M of all earnings. Confirm current terms at build time (V at implementation).
  • [ ] Set prices in store price points, not converted FX. A $9.99 US price is not $9.99 in Mexico.
  • [ ] Model the fee cliff: crossing $1M resets iOS to 30% until the following year. The Y3 margin in §7 already assumes it.

9.3 Trials, renewals, refunds

  • [ ] 7-day free trial via an introductory offer, one per subscriber per subscription group.
  • [ ] Paywall shows: price, period, trial length, renewal terms, cancellation path. All four, in the sheet, not behind a link.
  • [ ] No pre-selected consent, no "continue" that silently subscribes, no trial that converts without an email/receipt.
  • [ ] Restore Purchases on the paywall and in Settings (required).
  • [ ] Manage subscription deep-links to the OS settings page. We never say "cancel here".
  • [ ] Refunds are handled by Apple/Google. In-app copy: "Refunds are handled by the App Store." No promises, no support tickets we cannot action.
  • [ ] Price increases require consent on Apple. Never migrate a user to a higher tier silently.

9.4 App review and privacy

  • [ ] Account deletion in-app (Guideline 5.1.1(v)) — and it must delete audio, transcripts, and the score.
  • [ ] Sign in with Apple if any third-party sign-in exists (4.8). At launch: phone OTP only, so this does not apply until Google/Apple sign-in ships.
  • [ ] Privacy labels: Audio Data, Usage Data, Diagnostics, Purchase History. Declare "Used for App Functionality" and "Not Linked to You" only where true. Audio used for moderation is linked.
  • [ ] 18+ rating, no unrestricted web access, no user-generated public profiles.
  • [ ] Report/block reachable in one tap from the room — reviewers test this.
  • [ ] Demo account with an active subscription for review, plus a note explaining the 15-minute room (reviewers time out otherwise).

9.5 The one that gets apps rejected

Selling "expert access" without delivering it. If the Expert room is empty at review time, the reviewer sees a paid feature that does nothing. Ship a seeded Expert room during review hours and document it in the review notes. This is the most common rejection for marketplace-style apps.

11. What I think will kill this

Ordered by how fast it does the job.

1. Group-first liquidity (most likely). Four people per session across five topics is a hard matching problem. If P90 time-to-room stays above 45 seconds, the "instant" promise dies and the app becomes a scheduled event product. Signal: P90 > 45s at week 6. Action: cut to three topics, add AI co-host to free rooms, or move to scheduled tables.

2. The $9.99 trap. At 5% conversion the product is roughly break-even before fixed costs; at 3% it loses $72k/month at 250k MAU. The brief's conversion target is below the break-even line. Signal: conversion < 6% at month 12. Action: reprice Plus to $11.99 before cutting the free tier — price is the honest lever, and the cap mostly hits enthusiasts.

3. The Productivity Score. Hidden behavioural scoring invites three failures: bias against quiet and non-native speakers, brigading by two coordinated reporters, and a GDPR problem the moment an access restriction is automated. Signal: score-affected users appeal at >2%/month, or upheld-report rate diverges by accent/nationality in audit. Action: freeze score-based gating, move to human review only.

4. A safety incident that the sample missed. Sampled ASR means we do not hear most rooms. A viral clip of harassment in a "safe" app is worse than the same clip in an unsafe one, because the brand is the promise. Signal: any P1 with no prior report. Action: raise sampling in the affected cohort immediately; publish the change.

5. Two products in one. Topic rooms and language practice have different retention curves, different content, and different price sensitivity. The pivot keeps both, and the risk is that neither gets enough density. Signal: "Practice" rooms < 15% of minutes by month 6. Action: fold them into Career rooms or spin them out.

6. AI cost drift. A model upgrade, a longer summary, or an always-on co-host can double COGS in a sprint. Signal: free AI COGS > $0.10/MAU/month. Action: the cap and the sampling rate are the circuit breakers; they are config, not code.

The one thing I would not compromise: no DMs. Every other decision here can be revisited with data. The absence of a private channel is the reason a woman can join a room with five strangers at 11pm, and it is the only feature HelloTalk cannot copy without dismantling its own retention loop.
