Principle: the free tier must be genuinely useful and the paid tier must sell time, priority, and proof — never safety, never the learning loop, never the ability to leave a bad room.

Metering safety is not a business model; it is a scandal.

Why $12.99 / $79.99: it is exact parity with HelloTalk VIP, verified from their store listing on 2026-11-15. Parity turns the comparison into one sentence — "same price, and it actually connects you." Undercutting at $9.99 signals a weaker product and leaves money on the table from the segment with the highest willingness to pay.

Why a $24.99 Career tier: job seekers are buying an outcome with an income attached. Anchoring against a human interview coach ($100–300/session) rather than against a language app is honest and leaves the premium tier structurally open. It is the only tier where the content (role/JD-specific rooms, rubric feedback) justifies a different price.

Annual pricing: $79.99 (49% off monthly equivalent) and $149.99 (50% off). Annual is the retention product; it should be the default toggle, with monthly one tap away — not hidden.

2. What is metered, and why that exact thing

Resource Metered? Reason
Live minutes Yes — the only metered thing It is the scarce, costly resource (human supply + relay)
Corrections No Metering feedback converts the free tier into a demo
Recap Basic free, full paid The recap is the habit; the depth is the product
AI partner 5 min/day free Enough to prove it works, not enough to replace the human product
Safety tools Never Charging for reporting would be indefensible
Leaving a room Never —

The cap is 20 minutes/day, not 20 per month. A daily cap lets a free user build a habit (three sessions a week at ten minutes) without ever feeling rationed across a month. It also bounds free cost predictably.

Cap UX: remaining time is shown on Home only, as a quiet line — "14 min left today". The cap is never enforced mid-conversation. If a call reaches the limit, the current room finishes and the next join is blocked, with the paywall offered after the recap, when the value is freshest.

3. Unit economics

Live audio is dramatically cheaper to run than an AI tutor. That is the structural advantage of this product and the reason the free tier can exist at all.

These are hypotheses until measured (H), but the shape is the point: the marginal cost of a paid live user is under a dollar a month. An AI tutor with 300 minutes of speech costs $3–5. That gap is why this business can afford a generous free tier and why the tutor plan could not.

Break-even: with ~$0.40/month cost per free user and ~$11 net per Plus subscriber, break-even conversion is ≈3.6% of monthly actives — roughly in line with the category median, not double it. That is a materially easier business than the AI-tutor model, and it is the strongest argument for building this instead.

What we deliberately do not do: trial-bait (a 3-day trial with a card is the highest-converting and highest-churn pattern), fake scarcity ("only 3 spots"), or paywalling the second session. The second session is where the habit forms; it must be free.

6. What we will not charge for

  • Reporting, blocking, or leaving a call.
  • Corrections or the recap's basic version.
  • Access to a human when the AI fails.
  • Removing ads (there are none).
  • "Verification badge" as a cosmetic upgrade — verification is a gate, not a purchase.
  • Priority in the human queue sold as skipping safety checks. Priority matching reorders people who have all passed the same gate; it never bypasses one.
